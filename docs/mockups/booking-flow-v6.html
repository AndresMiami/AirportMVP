Bounded three-step flow (v6)

The scope Codex approved: a rearrangement of the existing booking flow, not new products. Every control here already exists in production — the direction selector, the Railway address search, the MIA/FLL/PBI grid, date and time, the vehicle carousel, the optional flight number. What changed is where they sit, how they are styled, and that the map now travels with the passenger. Passenger-selected pickup time stays authoritative; nothing derives it.

In scope

  • Where & When merged into one screen — direction selector, address search, airport grid, date, time, then the map
  • Choose a Ride — the existing carousel with the same map moved below it
  • Confirm & Pay → Your Ride — summary, payment space, then status and edit tools
  • Optional flight number, shared with the driver only
  • Visual quieting: one accent per screen, larger borderless rows, price on the button

Deliberately out of scope

  • The entry/preset screen
  • Port of Miami and terminal-to-terminal routes
  • The Pickup/Destination route box and inferred direction
  • Saved places, Home, recents
  • Flight-derived pickup timing and international buffers
  • Anything needing a change to the quote contract

What actually moved

  • Two screens became one. Direction, address, airport, date and time now sit together, with the map underneath. No control was added or removed.
  • The map travels. One element, moved from under the time row to under the carousel — never rebuilt, so the route and zoom survive.
  • Confirm & Pay is a real step. Production books straight from the vehicle screen; here the summary, traveler, payment, promotion and notes get their own screen, and the same screen becomes Your Ride afterwards.
  • The price rides on the button (“Book Cadillac Escalade · $165”), so the separate total row is gone.
  • Flight number is optional and says so. “We share it with your driver. Your pickup time stays exactly as you set it.”

Visual quieting

  • One accent colour per screen — the primary button. Selected direction, airport and date are white on gray instead of four different orange treatments.
  • Rows are 56 pt with 15 pt text and no 2 pt outlines; a validated address gets a checkmark rather than a green border.
  • Screen titles replace the progress dots; the summary bar still names where you are.
  • Terms/Privacy and Sign out sit below the card instead of floating over the button, which they do in production on a 393-pt phone.
  • Stepper labels shortened so they stop colliding; cancel demoted to a text link.

Held back for later, on purpose

  • Flight-derived pickup timing. The passenger’s chosen time stays authoritative. The buffer question is unresolved — the plan in the repo says 35–40 min plus 30 for customs in one place and 45 min in another — and it belongs with dispatch, not a form.
  • Port of Miami and terminal-to-terminal. A quote is keyed to one airport code plus one place id, so these need a contract change before they can be a screen.
  • The entry/preset screen and saved places. Worth revisiting once the bounded flow is shipped and there is more than one product to list.
  • All of the above is preserved as a separate exploration rather than deleted.
