The entry screen

  • It names the journeys, not the product. “Book Airport Transfer” tells a passenger nothing they didn’t already assume. “I’m landing” and “I’m flying out” sell the difference — especially the first one, because a passenger has no idea LinkMia will handle their flight until you say so.
  • Presets, not modes. Each card pre-fills the route box and opens the right field. Nothing is locked: the swap button, both fields and the saved list all still work, so a wrong tap costs one correction rather than a trip back.
  • The third card is the escape and the seam where this becomes a services page. Port of Miami today; hourly, tours and city-to-city as they ship. It becomes a dashboard by gaining products, not by gaining UI in advance.
  • Carrying the intent through /login.html is one query parameter the booking page reads on boot, the same way ?book=1 already works.

The arrival flow, with no flight feed

  • The flight replaces the time question. When the pickup is an airport, “Pickup time” becomes “Your flight”. Asking a landing passenger to choose a pickup time is asking them to do arithmetic the app should do.
  • Two things off their ticket: flight number and landing time. LinkMia adds the buffer — 30 minutes domestic, 45 international — and states the answer: we’ll have your driver there at 5:15 PM. No API, no subscription, useful today.
  • When the feed lands, the screen doesn’t change shape. The landing time stops being typed and starts being fetched, and “Arriving from” disappears because the origin airport implies it. One value changes source.
  • Only what’s true is printed. Today: “we’ll share your flight with your driver.” Not “we adjust automatically” — that sentence moves a driver’s committed schedule and can’t appear until the driver side is wired.
  • Always an escape. “I don’t know my flight yet” drops to a normal time picker, and the plain time row offers the flight path back. Nobody is trapped behind a flight number.

What it asks of the backend

  • Nothing, to ship the manual version. Flight number and landing time are two more stored fields; the pickup time is what it always was.
  • The buffer is a business rule, not a display. It decides when the driver is committed, so it belongs with pricing and dispatch, not in the browser.
  • “Automatic adjustment” is dispatch work. A moved landing time is an edit to a confirmed booking plus a driver push; a four-hour delay is a Release Ride event. The reserved /api/track-flight endpoint is the front door, but the schedule side is the real build.
  • Route-based flight lookup costs more than flight-number lookup. Listing every flight from MIA on a date is a schedules feed; one flight number is a single call. Ship the cheap one first — the picker is a later upgrade to the same screen.
