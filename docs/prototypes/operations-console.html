What "live" honestly means here

These markers are verified checkpoints, not a moving dot. A driver's phone captures one GPS fix at each of three taps — On my way, Arrived, Start trip — and that point is stamped with the status. Continuous background tracking was built once and deliberately removed: a mobile browser cannot keep broadcasting while Google Maps is in the foreground. A true moving dot needs a small native driver app, and that is a deliberate decision with a real cost, not an oversight. Until then the honest promise is "last verified at 4:12 PM, on the way" — never a live trace the product cannot actually deliver.

The blocker is the platform, not the code

Continuous browser geolocation was built once and deliberately removed. A mobile web page cannot keep reporting position once the driver switches to Google Maps for turn-by-turn — which is the first thing every driver does after accepting. The page is suspended, the stream stops, and the map silently freezes on a stale point while looking live. That failure mode is worse than no map at all, because the passenger sees it too.

One rule that must survive if this ships

Continuous positions are convenience. The three verified checkpoints stay the record. When there is a dispute about whether a driver was at the curb, the answer comes from the checkpoint stamped atomically with the status change — not from a stream of unauthenticated coordinates. A no-show still needs an Arrived checkpoint, elapsed waiting time, an attempted contact and an explicit approval. Live tracking must never become the evidence.

Drivers

A live inconsistency this screen surfaces

The drivers table has a commission_rate column and every driver is set to 0.75. But payout is not read from it — driver_payout is a generated database column hard-coded as price * 0.75. Today the two agree, so nothing is visibly wrong. The day a driver is put on a different rate, this screen will show one number and the ledger will pay another. Fixing it means freezing payout into a plain column at completion rather than deriving it forever.

Corporate accounts

Not built

This is the Uber-for-Business shape: a company, its people, a spend policy, central billing, and a negotiated rate. Here is exactly how far the system currently gets, so nobody designs against a fiction.

The blocker is not storage — it is that a discount cannot be expressed

Adjustment multipliers are validated as 1 ≤ rate ≤ 10. A 15% corporate discount is not a number the rate card can hold. A negotiated account needs either a whole alternate price list, or a new markup step in the engine. Both are code. See Can't change yet.

What this screen cannot yet tell you

Bookings do not store the distance, duration or rate-card version they were priced with. So "revenue by mile", "margin by route" and any before/after comparison across a price change are unavailable — not because the chart is missing, but because the facts were never written down. Storing four fields on each booking at the moment of pricing turns this page from a summary into an analysis.

Pricing Studio

Can't change yet

The safest thing this product does is refuse. Every setting below is something an operator reasonably expects. None exist in the engine today — so none get a text box that pretends otherwise.

Why refusing matters more here than in most tools

The card validator checks values, not field names. I published a card carrying minimumFareCents: 8500 and it was accepted, deep-frozen and registered as valid — then a 3-mile ride quoted $19.00. A dashboard that let you type a minimum fare would show a green check and change nothing. Every field in this product has to be one the engine provably reads.

Pre-publish check

Nothing to publish

The four guarantees

1 · An accepted booking never reprices

Publishing writes a new card version. It never runs an UPDATE against bookings. That is the easy half. The hard half is that driver_payout and linkmia_commission are GENERATED ALWAYS columns derived from bookings.price — so any future feature that edits a price silently rewrites the driver's historical payout and LinkMia's historical revenue, with no audit trail. The freeze has to be a stored snapshot, not a derivation.

2 · A quote already in a passenger's hands is honored for 15 minutes

Signed quotes carry the price and card version and live 15 minutes. Publish at 14:03 and a quote issued at 14:00 is still good until 14:15 — a price hold, and correct. But it means a typo'd card is live for up to 15 minutes after you fix it. Publishing needs "invalidate quotes already issued", which requires issued quotes stored server-side.

3 · Verification has to check the version against the database, not itself

The token verifier's expectation list is purpose · authUserId · customerId · vehicle · intentHash and it rejects any other expectation. So "compare the token's card version against the verified payload" is comparing a value to itself. The real check: that version id must exist, must have been live when the token was issued, and must not be revoked.

4 · The price charged is the price quoted

Today create-booking accepts price from the browser and writes it verbatim. Until that changes, a publish button is a control panel wired to nothing. Enforcement comes first; publishing comes after.

Design notes

Why this is shaped the way it is, and where I disagree with the brief.

The thesis: this engine is not merely complex, it is sometimes backwards

Raising every Tesla per-mile rate by 10% — one edit, one direction — produces this across five real Miami routes. Every number came from running the engine.

One route falls 8.2% from a price increase. One does not move at all. The spread on a single "+10%" is −8.2% to +52.6%. No amount of careful form design fixes this; only showing the consequence does. That is why the fare board, not a rate table, is the pricing home screen.

Why the console is shaped this way

The brief asked for what Uber for Business shows and what Moovs and Limo Anywhere control. Those are three different products, and taking all of them literally would produce a fourth that does none well. What each is actually good at:

  • Uber for Business — the buyer's view: spend, policy, who rode, receipts. That maps to Accounts and Insights.
  • Moovs / Limo Anywhere — the operator's view: dispatch board, driver assignment, fleet, reservations. That maps to Today, Rides, Fleet.
  • Neither does the thing LinkMia most needs — showing an operator what a price change actually does. That stays the centerpiece.

The dispatch board deliberately has no drag-and-drop

In Moovs you drag a trip onto a driver. LinkMia's dispatch model is the opposite by design: requests are shared, drivers accept, and acceptance stamps ownership atomically so two drivers can never take the same ride. A dispatcher who could drag a ride onto a driver would be racing the driver app. The board shows state and lets you intervene; it does not pretend to be the source of truth.

"Real-time tracking" is designed as checkpoints, not a moving dot

Continuous browser GPS was built and removed once already — it cannot survive the handoff to Google Maps. Drawing a live trace would be the single most dishonest thing this console could do, because passengers would see it too. The map shows three verified points with honest timestamps. A real moving dot needs a native driver shell; that is a business decision with a price tag, and it belongs on a roadmap, not hidden behind a spinner.

Where I disagree with the brief

"Change Premier SUV from $160/hour to $175/hour" doesn't map onto a real setting

LinkMia has no hourly product. The nearest real field is the Escalade's slow-traffic floor, currently $125/hr. Raising it to $175/hr changes nothing on MIA→Brickell, FLL→Miami or PBI→Miami — it only touches short or badly delayed trips. Run it on Try a change.

"Fixed MIA→Brickell prices" cannot be fixed, twice over

Fixed-route pricing is dead code on both paths — the browser sends a Google place_id, and the server endpoint doesn't pass route codes at all. And even reached, a fixed rate is not final: type $150 and the engine adds the airport fee and re-rounds to $159; that same $200 Escalade rate bills $269 on a Saturday night.

Don't build stored publication for v0.1

Git already gives immutable versions, authorship, a reason, review, history and rollback — and that workflow already runs here. The hard part was never storing the card; it was knowing what a number does. Ship the board and the simulator, output a diff for a pull request, and build stored publication when someone who can't use git needs to change a price, or the first negotiated account exists.

Build order

  • Now, read-only: fare board, try-a-change with the wrong-way gate, why-this-price, can't-change-yet. Writes nothing, ships before enforcement.
  • Next: browser integration and endpoint enforcement, so the price charged is the price quoted. Everything else depends on this.
  • Then: store route facts and card version on each booking — this is what turns why-this-price from a re-simulation into a record, and Insights from a summary into an analysis.
  • Then: Today / Rides / Fleet on real data. Every field they need already exists.
  • Later, on a trigger: stored publication, corporate accounts, negotiated rates.
  • Only if the business needs it: the native driver app that makes a moving dot honest.

One rule that came out of building this

Every number on every screen must be produced by running the engine, never typed by hand — including examples in documentation. Of three independent designs drafted for this exercise, the two that hand-authored their headline examples both shipped fabricated figures presented as verified; the one that ran the engine got them right.

Prototype scope. Self-contained page. No network calls, no credentials, no Supabase, no LinkMia API. It carries a copy of the pricing engine's arithmetic, checked cent-for-cent against backend/functions/lib/ride-quote.js across 5,040 route × vehicle × time combinations with zero mismatches. Route distances and durations are the real traffic-aware figures measured during the quote-service rollout. Driver, ambassador and booking record shapes match the live schema; the day's rides are synthetic. Nothing in the live application was modified.
